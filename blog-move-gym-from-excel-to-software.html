How to Move Your Gym from a Register or Excel to Software (Without Losing Data)

Over the past months I've visited 110 gyms across Assam, showing owners what gym management software can do. Almost nobody told me the software itself was a bad idea. Most owners liked the WhatsApp reminders straight away. What stopped them was the move.

They had a register with years of renewals written in it, an Excel sheet the manager kept, a WhatsApp group of members, and payments scattered across their phone. Typing all of that into something new sounded like a week of work. And many owners were — fairly — not comfortable handing their member list to someone they had just met.

Both worries are reasonable. This guide is how to make the switch yourself, in an evening, without losing anyone and without giving your data to an outsider.

What a gym's member data really looks like

Before you start, it helps to know that your data is not unusually messy. In the gyms I've visited, member records almost always live in some mix of:

  • A paper register, where each renewal is a new line — so the same member appears again and again.
  • An Excel or Google Sheet, usually kept by the manager rather than the owner.
  • A WhatsApp group or chat history, which is where most reminders and payment screenshots end up.
  • UPI payments on someone's phone, matched to members from memory.

Dates are written in different ways ("5 June", "05/06/25", "June 2025"), phone numbers sit in a remarks column, and nobody is quite sure who has expired. That's normal. Good software should deal with it for you rather than make you clean it first.

Step 1: Decide who will actually use it

At one gym I worked with, the owner loved the reminders — but it was the manager who entered every member and recorded every payment. If the manager hadn't been part of the switch, it would have stalled. It took them about two days to feel comfortable.

So before importing anything, sit down with whoever runs the front desk day to day. They are the person who has to find a member in five seconds when someone walks in. If they're comfortable, the switch sticks.

Step 2: Start with five members, not five hundred

Don't begin with your whole list. Add five members by hand — people whose plans end in the next week or two — and switch on renewal reminders. Watch one reminder go out on WhatsApp and see what your member receives.

This takes ten minutes and answers the real question ("does this work for my gym?") before you've invested an evening. If it doesn't feel right, you've lost nothing.

Step 3: Put everything into one sheet

Once you're happy, gather your members into a single Excel or CSV file. It doesn't need to be tidy. These are the columns that matter:

  • Name
  • Phone number — the most important column by far. It's how reminders reach people. A member without a phone number is a member you can't remind.
  • Plan — Monthly, Quarterly, Half-yearly, Yearly…
  • Expiry date, or the date of their last payment.

If your register has the same person on twelve lines because they renewed every month, leave them all in. Don't delete anything by hand.

Step 4: Let the software do the cleaning

This is where most owners expect pain, and where it should be easiest. When you drop your file into GymExec's import, it:

  • reads Excel and CSV files, including messy ones;
  • understands mixed date formats in the same column;
  • merges a member's repeated renewal rows into one member;
  • looks for phone numbers even in oddly named columns, and warns you if it finds almost none;
  • works out each member's plan from their dates;
  • flags members who look like duplicates.

You don't reformat anything first. That's the point.

Step 5: Review on screen before anything is saved

Nothing should go into your gym's records until you've seen it. GymExec shows every member on screen first, so you can add a missing phone number, fix a name, or drop someone who left years ago. Members with no phone number are clearly marked, so you can fill those in from your phone contacts before you import.

Step 6: Import — and keep the register for one cycle

When it looks right, import. From that moment your members are tracked, you can see who is expiring this week, and reminders start going out on their own.

My advice: keep your paper register running alongside for one renewal cycle — about a month. Compare the two at the end. Once you trust the software, retire the notebook. And if you spot something wrong later, fix the sheet and re-import: GymExec matches members by phone number, so you won't end up with duplicates.

"But I don't want to give my member data to anyone"

I heard this from many owners, and I understand it. Your members trusted you with their phone numbers. With GymExec you don't hand your list to us — you upload it yourself, from your own phone or laptop, and you can export everything back to Excel whenever you like. Your original sheet stays with you as a backup the whole time.

Mistakes I've seen owners make

  • Importing members without phone numbers. They show up in the software, but nobody can remind them. Spend ten minutes filling the gaps first.
  • Treating everyone as active. Make sure expiry dates come across, so people who stopped coming months ago show as expired — not as members you're failing to remind.
  • Switching everything on day one. Start with reminders. Attendance, staff and the rest can come later, once the front desk is comfortable.
  • Doing it without the person at the front desk. See step 1.

Why it's worth the evening

Once your members are in, the software can do the job a register never could: remind each member before their plan runs out, on WhatsApp, without anyone remembering to send it. In my experience that's the feature owners care about most — because every member who renews instead of quietly drifting away is money you would otherwise have lost.
